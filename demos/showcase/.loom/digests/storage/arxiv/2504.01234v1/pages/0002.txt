sum of arrow divergences along a path, and the vectors eu − ev generate the lattice of vectors
summing to zero on each component. The rank statement follows by counting coordinates.
Lemma 2.2. The cycle space of a quiver is a saturated sublattice of the lattice of weightings:
if nw is balanced for some nonzero integer n, then w is balanced.
Proof. The divergence map is a homomorphism of free abelian groups, and ZV (Q) is torsionfree, so n∂w = ∂(nw) = 0 implies ∂w = 0.

3

Rank and duality

We can now compute the rank of the cycle space, which is the invariant the rest of the literature
calls the cycle rank or the first Betti number of the underlying graph.
Theorem 3.1. Let Q be a quiver whose underlying graph has c connected components. Then
the cycle space of Q is a free abelian group of rank |A(Q)| − |V (Q)| + c, and this rank depends
only on the underlying graph of Q.
Proof. The cycle space is the kernel of the divergence map, whose image has rank |V (Q)| − c
by Proposition 2.1. Since the domain has rank |A(Q)|, the rank of the kernel is the difference.
Reversing an arrow changes the sign of the corresponding coordinate and is an automorphism
of the domain carrying one cycle space onto the other, so the rank is unchanged.
Corollary 3.2. The cycle space of a quiver is trivial if and only if the underlying graph is a
forest.
Proof. A graph with c components is a forest exactly when it has |V (Q)| − c edges, which
by Theorem 3.1 is exactly the condition that the rank of the cycle space vanish. A saturated
sublattice of rank zero is trivial by Lemma 2.2.

4

Examples and remarks

The bouquet of n loops at a single vertex has cycle space Zn , and every weighting of it is
balanced. A single arrow between two distinct vertices has trivial cycle space. A directed cycle
of length ℓ has cycle space Z, generated by the weighting that is 1 on every arrow of the cycle.
Remark 4.1. Nothing above uses the orientation of the arrows except as bookkeeping, which
is the content of the last sentence of the proof of Theorem 3.1. Readers who prefer graphs to
quivers may replace every quiver by its underlying graph with an arbitrary orientation, and
no statement changes.
Remark 4.2. For an infinite quiver of finite valence the divergence map is still defined on finitely
supported weightings and its kernel is still saturated, but the rank formula of Theorem 3.1 has
no meaning, since all three of its terms are infinite. The correct statement in that setting is a
description of the cycle space as a direct limit, which we do not pursue.
