5

Balanced subquivers

A subquiver P of Q is given by subsets of the vertices and arrows of Q such that every arrow
of P has its source and its target in P . A weighting of P extends by zero to a weighting of Q,
and the question of this section is when balance survives the extension.
Theorem 5.1. Let P be a subquiver of a quiver Q and w a weighting of P . The extension of
w by zero is balanced on Q if and only if w is balanced on P . In particular the cycle space of
P is a saturated sublattice of the cycle space of Q, of rank |A(P )| − |V (P )| + c(P ).
Proof. The divergence of the extension at a vertex of P is the divergence of w there, since
every arrow carrying a nonzero weight lies in P ; at a vertex outside P it is zero, since no arrow
of P meets such a vertex. So the extension is balanced exactly when w is. Extension by zero
is an injective homomorphism whose image is cut out by the vanishing of the coordinates of
the arrows outside P , so the image is saturated, and Lemma 2.2 applied to P gives saturation
inside the cycle space of Q. The rank is Theorem 3.1 for P .
Corollary 5.2. A weighting of Q supported on a subquiver whose underlying graph is a forest
is balanced only if it is zero.
Proof. Such a weighting is the extension by zero of a weighting of the subquiver, which by
Theorem 5.1 is balanced on it, and the cycle space of a forest is trivial by Corollary 3.2.
The criterion is what makes the cycle space local: a balanced weighting may be studied
one subquiver at a time, provided the subquivers together carry its support. We record the
consequence used most often, that the cycle space of a disjoint union is the direct sum of the
cycle spaces of its parts, without separate proof.
Remark 5.3. The converse of Corollary 5.2 fails as soon as the support contains a cycle: the
weighting that is 1 on every arrow of a directed cycle and 0 elsewhere is balanced and nonzero.
What the corollary rules out is a balanced weighting whose support is acyclic, and that is the
form in which Theorem 3.1 is usually applied.
