The hypothesis is needed

Let X = {a, b} carry the indiscrete topology and let σ exchange a and b; the fixed locus
is empty, hence closed, so this is not yet a counterexample. Instead let X = {a, b, c} with
open sets ∅, {a, b} and X, and let σ fix c and exchange a with b. Then Fix(σ) = {c}, whose
complement {a, b} is open, so the locus is closed here too.
A genuine counterexample needs a space that is not T1 in a more substantial way, and we
give one in the line with two origins, where the two origins are exchanged by an involution
fixing everything else. The fixed locus is the complement of the two origins, which is not closed.
