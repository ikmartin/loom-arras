# The reference layer, tested on a real bibliography: what four iterations found

2026-09-19. Plan 0.12 built a reference layer for a quilt, meaning the papers a quilt cites turned into something an agent can ground itself in and an author can navigate. It then tested the layer the way its §15 prescribed. Each iteration built a fresh copy of the relative-localization paper, seeded it with the author's bibliography and 25 PDFs, and let three agents work in it while I played the author. I then investigated what happened, wrote a change list and fixed it. This report is for the author. It says what the study found, not what was built.

**Scope, stated first.** The plan asked for five iterations. **Iterations 1–3 ran in full. Iteration 4 was stopped** after its first run had answered one question and filed seven proposals, and **iteration 5 was not run**, both by the author's decision on 2026-09-19. Every table below therefore has four rows, the last one partial. §5 of this report says what that leaves untested.

**How to read this.** Every item carries a verdict: **FIX** (with the change, and whether it is done), **DISCARD** (with the reason) or **PASS** (it worked). Items are grouped by root cause, not by finding number. Each claim is marked **verified** (I reproduced it: read the page, ran the command, rendered the image) or **reported** (an agent's word, not reproduced). The raw findings, change lists and measurement notes live in the session scratchpad (`scratchpad/012/iter{1..4}/`, `measurements.md`, `PROGRESS.md`); this report is the durable record.

## 1. The four answers §16.3 requires

**1. Is the gradient real?** No, not on this evidence. The data does not show later questions getting cheaper. The same localization question was asked early and late in iterations 2 and 3, and early in iteration 4:

| | early | late |
|---|---|---|
| iteration 2 | oriented, cold corpus: 46 calls, 632 s, 152k tokens | plain agent: 30 calls, 465 s, 127k |
| iteration 3 | plain agent: 28 calls, 334 s, 128k | oriented, reattached, 13 verified results and 7 links: 35 calls, 544 s, 150k |
| iteration 4 | oriented: 39 calls, 605 s, 140k | not run |

Each pair changes two things at once (the agent's orientation and the corpus's richness), so neither pair measures the gradient. Iteration 4 was the first pair with orientation held fixed, and its late half was never run. What the numbers do show is a different fact: **plain agents answer this question more cheaply than oriented ones** (28 and 30 calls, against 35 to 46). They do it by reading `results.json` and `pages/` directly, never running loom to read (**verified**, iterations 1–3). **The one clean instance of a cheaper answer** is iteration 2. An oriented agent, asked to trace Chang–Kiem–Li's Theorem 3.4 through the literature in both directions, used links recorded earlier in the same study and answered in **12 calls, where the equivalent question from the papers had taken 46** (**verified**). So a later question costs less when the earlier work left links. The plan's broader claim is not shown.

**2. Did proposals needing an edit fall?** The rate fell in the kinds of mistake loom can see, and a different kind showed through: misreadings that only the page image reveals.

| | proposals | needed an edit | what the edits were |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 12 (3 reviewed) | 0 of 3 | none, because the surface made them impossible to judge: a one-clause quote beside a ten-line rendering (see `both-texts-iter1.png`) |
| 2 | 13 intended (26 filed) | 6 of 13 | 5 glosses added by the agent, 1 clause dropped at a page break; **13 of 13** first attempts wrapped their own environment |
| 3 | 16 | **7 of 16** | 4 glosses, 3 misreadings of notation the text layer had erased (𝒳 for X, σ∩τ for σ⁰, G for T), GP's formula written from memory; 0 malformed |
| 4 | 7 (stopped) | not reviewed | 0 of 7 carry a word the quoted text lacks, 0 malformed, 7 of 7 named by the paper's own numbers |

The glosses and the malformed statements did fall, but for mechanical reasons, not because the guidance changed. **A line in the orientation reduced neither.** Iteration 3's agent repeated two of iteration 2's glosses word for word after the orientation told it not to. A malformed statement stopped when `propose` began refusing it, and glosses stopped reaching the author when `propose` began naming the words the quote does not contain. **What is left is misreading, and no text check can find it.** The PDF's text layer keeps one "X" for a stack and its coarse space, loses every superscript and subscript, and turns display formulae into control bytes. §5 of the plan assumed the page text was the page. It is not, and **that assumption was wrong in a way no individual bug report names**. The plan's §16.3 asks the report to say exactly that.

**The strongest evidence is against the author, not the agents.** In iteration 3 I reviewed thirteen Brion statements against the page *text*, as the author, and made seven edits. Iteration 4's screenshot then put the page image beside the same theorem, and it shows that **two of my seven edits removed the paper's own words**. Theorem 3.4's "∈ S^m_Q" and Theorem 4.5's "where χ₁,…,χₙ are the weights of x" are both on the page, and the text layer had dropped or garbled both (**verified**: `page-image.png`, and pages 15 and 20 rendered). Iteration 2's author had verified Theorem 3.4 as written, which was correct. A text-only verification surface let the author introduce errors into verified results, and this is the measured reason the page image now stands under both texts.

**3. Claims the loop falsified.** Four. The first three have decision records (DR-179, DR-180; §6). The fourth is not shown rather than disproved.
- **§4.3, "a mechanical digest … is correct by construction."** Behrend–Fantechi's was silently mis-numbered (its `lem-3.6` was the paper's Lemma 3.8). Romagny 2022 extracted 1 result of 27 and Totaro 0 of 13. And Chang–Kiem–Li's was extracted faithfully from the preprint while the author cites the published version, where the preprint's Theorem 3.4 is Theorem 3.5 and its conclusion lives in A^T_* X(σ), not A^T_* X (**verified**, `ckl-published-p18.png`). A mechanical digest is correct about the file it parsed and nothing more.
- **§5.3, "verification shows both texts" as the non-negotiable.** Both texts were shown, and wrong renderings passed anyway. The page *image* is the missing third element.
- **§5.4, "an author's edited statement … does not need to be" re-verified, because "the author is the authority."** The author made 2 errors in 7 edits by trusting the text layer. The author is the authority on what they want, not on what a text layer says a page says.
- **§13, "the next question in this area is cheaper than the last."** Not shown (answer 1).

**4. What was not tested.** See §5 below, which lists it in full: the whole of iteration 5, the second half of iteration 4, the gradient with orientation held fixed, §5.2's central bet, and the review of iteration 4's proposals.

## 2. The findings, by root cause

### A. The text layer is not the page

**Findings:** iteration 3 R1-11, R2-6, R2-16, S-3; iteration 4's page-image correction. **Verdict: FIX, done.**

Three kinds of evidence, all **verified** by rendering the page:
- **A hypothesis moved from the space to the stack.** Alper's Theorem 7.5.11 reads "if π : 𝒳 → X is a good moduli space with X quasi-separated". Its text layer prints three identical X's. Iteration 2's agent rendered the hypothesis on X, iteration 3's on 𝒳. The anchor check passed both, because the page text does say "X" (`alper-p354.png`).
- **Two Brion statements misread.** In Theorem 5.4 the text layer's σ⁰ became σ∩τ, and in Theorem 7.3 A^T became A^G (`brion-p24.png`). Iteration 2's agent had both right.
- **A formula written from memory.** Graber–Pandharipande's main result is their equation (1), and in the PDF's text layer it is control bytes. The agent quoted the prose before it, which stops at "The localization formula is th", and wrote the formula from what the prose describes. It came out as Σᵢ ι_{i*}(…) where the paper has ι_* Σ. The paper's LaTeX source was on disk the whole time.

What changed:
- `loom build` renders the anchor page of every pending proposal from the local PDF. The image is never committed and is checked against the artifact's hash first.
- The proposal box shows it open under the two texts, scrolled to where the quote begins, with a mark in the margin (`page-image.png`). The position comes from pdftotext's word boxes and placed 15 of 16 anchors in the iteration-3 quilt.
- For a work with a LaTeX source, `loom refs propose --source-file` anchors the quotation in the source (contract §9.3, amended), so the source text is the LaTeX itself.

**An honest note on the scroll.** Iteration 4's first screenshots showed no scroll. That turned out to be an artefact of Playwright's element screenshot, which re-lays a box taller than the viewport and resets its inner scroll. The live page does scroll, to 75–417 px (**verified** in the browser). I still made the scroll independent of image-load order, and an end-to-end test now asserts it.

### B. Agents gloss, and guidance does not stop them

**Findings:** iteration 2 A-2/A-3; iteration 3 R2-6, R2-7. **Verdict: FIX, done.**

In iteration 2, five of six edits were additions: "(Q the fraction field of S)", "for α a simple root", definitions of symbols the statement does not define. The orientation gained a sentence ("the paper's words only"). In iteration 3, 4 of 13 statements carried glosses anyway, two of them iteration 2's word for word, and I let one through in a careful review. `words_not_on_page` now names the prose words of a rendering that its quote does not contain. Mathematics and naming commands are excluded.
- **Measured on iteration 3's real proposals:** it flagged all 4 real glosses and gave no false positive on the statements that had none, after fixes for `\begin{…}` arguments and words hyphenated across a line (**verified**). A fifth item I had counted as a gloss, Theorem 3.4's "∈ S^m_Q", went unflagged. That was correct, because it is the paper's own clause (§1, answer 2).
- **Iteration 4:** 0 of 7 statements carried an unquoted word. `propose` prints the words to the agent, and the proposal box lists them for the author (`words-not-on-page.png`).

### C. Malformed and misnamed proposals

**Findings:** iteration 2 R1-14/R2-10, R1-15; iteration 3 R2-4, R2-17. **Verdict: FIX, done.**

- **Wrapped statements:** 13 of 13 first attempts in iteration 2 wrapped their own `\begin{theorem}…\label`, one after reading a warning about exactly that. The fix is a refusal, not more guidance. Iteration 3: 0 of 16. Iteration 4: 0 of 7.
- **Invented names:** Brion numbers corollaries within a subsection, and iteration 3's agent invented `cor-2.3-quotient`. `--local` must now be the paper's own number (`cor-2.3.1`), and the taxon is read off it. Iteration 4's agent named all seven correctly.
- **Equations:** GP's main result is a numbered display with no theorem around it, so `--taxon equation` gives it the locator "Equation (1)", written into the quilt's theorem environment. This answers the plan's open question D5, and contract §3.2 is amended to match.
- **Environments:** every proposal is now written into the quilt's own environment for its taxon, by display name. A `corollary` in a quilt with no corollary environment was not a node at all.

### D. One word for two claims

**Findings:** iteration 1 R2-11, R1-8/R1-19; iteration 3 R2-9, R2-21. **Verdict: FIX, done.**

- **The agent said "All 13 verified" to the author** about proposals nobody had looked at. It was repeating loom's own help text, which said "verified against the page". The contract said it too: its §9.5 called the anchor check "verification", while §9.9's `verified` state means a person accepted the rendering.
  - The check is now "checked" everywhere an agent reads it.
  - Under an agent marker, `propose` ends "waiting for the author" instead of printing the author's command.
  - Contract §9.5 is amended.
- **Iteration 1:** an agent verified its own proposal and loom recorded the author as the verifier, because an agent's shell shares the author's git identity. The author's verbs (`loom accept`, `refs verify`, `refs discard`) now refuse under an agent marker. `loom accept` refuses external keys outright, and `refs verify` owns the transcription claim (plan decision C).

### E. A mechanical digest is correct about the file it parsed, and nothing more

**Findings:** iteration 1 R2-12, R1-3, R1-3b; iteration 3 R3-9, R1-2/R1-4/R2-14; iteration 4 R1-3. **Verdict: FIX, mostly done; one part open.**

**Extraction gaps** (all fixed, with tests):
- Papers declaring results with `\newenvironment` wrappers around a counter: Behrend–Fantechi went from 44 results to 72, with exact numbering, and Romagny from 1 to 65.
- Theorems declared after `\begin{document}`: Totaro went from 0 to 14.
- Numbers partly counted rather than compiled now say `numbering: mixed`.
- Empty shells are flagged as thin.

**Standing assumptions:** until iteration 3 these were always an `\incomplete` stub, and five sightings across the iterations showed that stub was the limit every hypothesis answer ran into. The extractor now fills the `-setup` node from a conventions heading, or gathers the sentences that state an assumption and says it gathered them. That covers 11 of 16 works in the study, 0 before, including Edidin–Graham's "the coefficients of all Chow groups are rational" (**verified**).

**The version problem, a regression this plan introduced (★★★★).** DR-109 exists to warn when a digest was extracted from a preprint while the bibliography cites the published version. 0.12's fetch puts a source fetched on a candidate arXiv id into the directory named for the work's DOI. The extractor named the artifact after that directory, so every digest from a candidate named the work's directory instead of the preprint it was read from. For the 11 works with a DOI, **the digest claimed to be the published version, and the warning could not fire**.
- An agent found Chang–Kiem–Li's preprint numbering and different conclusion by auditing the digest against the PDF. It was the best single piece of agent work in the study.
- **Fixed:** fetch records which identifier the source came from (`src.json`). Iteration 4's fresh quilt raised `loom:unverified-locators` for 11 works, against 0 before (**verified**).
- **Open (iteration 4 R1-3):** the warning appears only in `loom lint`. Iteration 4's agent re-found the same discrepancy, filed it as an extraction bug and never saw the warning, because nothing it ran to read a reference shows it. A patch is drafted and not applied (WQ-37): a `preprint` marker in `refs coverage`, and a note from `loom source` and `refs overview`.
- **Proposed, not built (D8, WQ-39):** a mechanical version check that compares the number printed in the cited PDF with the digest's.

### F. Who did what

**Findings:** iteration 1 R2-11, SURF-6; iteration 2 R1-8, R2-3; iteration 3 R2-8. **Verdict: FIX, done.**

- Links were recorded as the author's (git's user name) when an agent wrote them. `refs link` now takes `--run` and refuses under an agent marker without one. Iteration 3: 7 of 7 links attributed to their runs.
- An author's edit was recorded as "edited by isaac" with no content. The proposed statement is now kept beside the verified one and `refs why` shows the diff.
- A second edit produced two diffs, both labelled "proposed". Now there is one diff, from what the agent proposed to what was verified.
- A run's name was stored in two spellings. It is now normalised.

### G. Where information is, and what points at it

**Findings:** iteration 1 R3-10, R3-12, R1-2, R1-20; iteration 2 R1-2/R3-6, R1-3, R2-2; iteration 3 R2-3, R2-5, R2-11/R3-7, R2-13, R2-15, R3-11. **Verdict: FIX, done; this is CLAUDE.md passes 1, 5 and 6 over and over.**

The recurring fault was loom's own documentation drifting behind loom, and some of the drift was mine:
- I added a "When there is no source" section to `ingest.md` in iteration 1 without removing the older one that contradicted it.
- I added `loom refs overview` in iteration 2 and left three documents saying no command prints an overview. Two agents then quoted the stale sentence as their reason for reading the file directly.

Fixed:
- one no-source section in ingest mode;
- three documents pointing at `refs overview`;
- the orientation now says `refs coverage` takes an author or title fragment (it had since iteration 1), because agents searched `refs.bib` with grep four times;
- `ai findings --run` shows the author's edits, because two agents learned them by diffing their own scratch scripts;
- `refs grep` refuses a regex, because `A\|B.*C` silently found nothing;
- read-only `refs` commands take `--run` and log to `run.log`.

The last one made "pages read per proposal" measurable for the first time: 3.4 in iteration 4.

### H. The author's side

**Findings:** iteration 3 R3-6, R2-6 (rename), S-2, S-5. **Verdict: FIX, done.**

- **Corrections never reached the document.** Re-verifying a result with a corrected statement updated `results.json` and never the digest file, so `loom source`, every bundle and the viewer kept the corrected text. An agent asked what the author changed reported the document faithfully and was wrong. It was found only because its answer disagreed with my memory.
- **The author could not rename a proposal.** Brion's "Theorem 3.2" is the paper's Corollary 3.2.1, and the name could not be fixed at verify time. It can now, from the CLI and the viewer.
- **Display math showed as source.** The proposal box printed a statement's display math as raw `$$…$$`, which hid the one formula GP's proposal existed for.
- **"Cited" meant two things.** `refs coverage` counted citations while the viewer counted citing keys.

### I. Speed, and a race I introduced

**Findings:** iteration 1 PERF; iteration 3 C-1. **Verdict: FIX, done (the race); open (diagrams).**

- **Speed:** with 16 digests the first `loom serve` build took 454 s, one LaTeX compile per diagram. Rendering fragments on a thread pool cut it to 156–176 s cold and 1.5 s warm.
- **The race (a regression, ★★★):** the pool shared the renderer's include stack and diagnostic list across threads. One thread expanding `sections/results.tex` made another thread's include of it look like a cycle, and diagnostics landed in finishing order. The fixture refreshed with a false `data-cycle`, and a rebuild did not reproduce it, which is how it was found. The state is now per-thread, and each job's diagnostics are merged in job order, so output equals a single-threaded build's (**verified**, with a regression test).
- **Open since iteration 1:** most digest diagrams do not render. The paper's own macros are missing from the digest's macro block, and `\CMcal` collides between the author's preamble and a digest's requirements (visible in `links.png`). Compiling a digest block against the paper's own preamble is a design change, not attempted.

### J. The views

**Findings:** iteration 1 SURF-1..5; iteration 2 links placement; plan §9. **Verdict: PASS, after fixes.**

- **Graph:** the seventh view works, and the graph now carries the author's 47 nodes out of 1,191 (`graph-after.png`). Before the fix, 226 digest section nodes were not marked external and leaked into it.
- **Links:** they sit at the top of a work's page. At the bottom of a 45-result digest, nobody found them.
- **Filtered list:** `?show=proposed` gives the list form of the same view (`bulk-filter.png`).

## 5. What was not tested, and why

- **Iteration 5, and iterations 4's runs 2 and 3.** Stopped by the author. Consequently:
  - The fixes made in iteration 3 were met by one agent query and seven proposals only. The source-file anchors and the equation taxon were **never used by an agent**.
  - The version-display patch (E) was never applied.
- **The gradient with orientation held fixed.** Iteration 4's order was chosen for it, and its late half was never run.
- **§5.2's bet**, that an author meets proposals in place rather than working a list. The plan says the loop cannot answer this, because its author is an AI, and it did not.
- **Iteration 4's seven proposals** were never reviewed against their page images, so iteration 4 has no edit rate.
- **Iteration 2's author edits** were made from the page text and never re-audited against images. That quilt was deleted, and iteration 3's audit shows such edits can be wrong.
- **Discards and re-proposals after a discard.** Iteration 1 had one discard and iteration 2's thirteen were duplicates. The discard-and-return path was exercised once, by an agent reading the reason on reattach.
- **`loom:cites-proposed-node`.** No author cited a proposed node.
- **A coauthor without the PDFs.** Anchors were re-checked from committed page text in iteration 1, but not by a second person or machine.
- **Every agent was the same model on medium thinking.** How other models behave is not known.
