**walk.md: the ten things that most need to explain themselves on screen** (in substance):

1. Whether an AI will read a given comment or annotation, and whether it already has.
2. Whether an AI is connected to this project now, and how to connect one, from the app.
3. Annotate mode: whether it is on, and that a selection becomes a comment only when it is.
4. What a session is, and what "writing in …" means for the person.
5. What Keep does to the file, and how to undo it in the same place.
6. Your Changes against Proposed Changes (the names are swapped from what they hold).
7. Accept (I vouch for a result) against Keep (take an edit into the text).
8. Which file is the paper (blue names, "-atomic", `main.tex`).
9. The margin markers and colours: a legend.
10. Problems in a mathematician's words, saying which ones a draft is expected to have, with a fix that is not a terminal command.

**walk.md: what a two-minute introduction would have to say that the screen could not** (in substance):

- loom is a reader beside your editor: you keep writing in your own editor, and loom shows the paper (the blue, rendered file), its PDF, the papers you cite, and everyone's annotations on it.
- Your AI assistant connects through a one-time `loom ai init` in a terminal in the project folder; then start Claude Code or Codex there. The app does not show whether it is connected.
- AIs read your annotations. Choose a type: Question or Revision for something you want answered or fixed, Note for a remark to yourself. To hand a passage to your AI directly, select it, press Quote, and paste.
- To annotate, turn on the pen+Ab button first; then a selection is marked and you can type a comment.
- An AI's suggested edits wait in Review › Your Changes. Keep writes them into your file; loom saves the previous version first, and you undo a Keep from The Record › Versions › Restore.
- Accept means "I have read this result and vouch for it"; Review › Your Changes lists results never accepted or changed since.
- Sessions are your AI conversations; their transcripts and what each did are kept in The Record (the walker never learned what choosing the session it was "writing in" changes).
- Problems lists what loom finds wrong in the files; most fixes happen in your editor, some need a terminal, and an open lemma without a proof is expected.
- Starting a new project, pairing with Overleaf and some fixes happen in a terminal, not in the app.

**walk-small.md: the five things that confused it most** (in substance): whether an assistant is connected and can see a comment (the bar, the "WRITING INTO" list and the Sessions table seem to disagree); undo for Keep (only a whole-file restore); adding a PDF (the list drop does nothing; the working "+" has no label); marking text (Quote, comment, highlight and note each do something different); problems fixable only in a terminal with commands it could not interpret.
