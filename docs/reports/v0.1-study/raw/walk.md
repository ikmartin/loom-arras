## Method and what to discount

- Viewport 1440×900 for A–D, then 1100×800 and 760×800 in fresh browser profiles for E. Every action was decided from the previous screenshot.
- Tooltips: native `title` tooltips are not painted in headless screenshots, so the driver reported the tooltip text of whatever was under the pointer. I counted a tooltip as seen only when I had deliberately hovered there. A real user hovers far less than I did: about a third of what I learned came from tooltips.
- I know what `.mcp.json`, `.gitignore` patterns, `git config`, MCP and Claude Code are. A typical mathematician may not, so the Settings › Agents page is easier for me than it will be for them.
- Dragging a file in and pasting were simulated with synthetic DOM events; they exercise the page's handlers but cannot show what a real browser does with a drop the page ignores (it may navigate away to the PDF).
- My actions changed the scratch copy of the showcase (two suggestions kept and one restored, one result accepted, one work added to the library, five comments and one note). Counts in later screenshots reflect that.
- Process note for the lead: early in setup I ran `pkill -f chromium_headless_shell-1243` to clear a stale driver of mine. If another tester's headless Chromium from the same Playwright install was running at about 20:14 UTC, it was killed. I did not repeat this, and DRIVER.md now warns against it.

## The ten things that most need to explain themselves on screen

1. Whether an AI will read a given comment or annotation, and whether it already has.
2. Whether an AI is connected to this project now, and how to connect one, from the app.
3. Annotate mode: whether it is on, and that a selection becomes a comment only when it is.
4. What a session is, and what "writing in …" means for me.
5. What Keep does to my file, and how to undo it in the same place.
6. Your Changes vs Proposed Changes (the names are swapped from what they hold).
7. Accept (I vouch for a result) vs Keep (take an edit into the text).
8. Which file is the paper (blue names, "-atomic", main.tex).
9. The margin markers and colours: a legend.
10. Problems in mathematician's words, with which ones a draft is expected to have and a fix that is not a terminal command.

## What a two-minute introduction would have to tell you that the screen could not

- loom is a reader beside your editor: you keep writing in your own editor, and loom shows the paper (the blue, rendered file), its PDF, the papers you cite, and everyone's annotations on it.
- Your AI assistant connects through a one-time `loom ai init` in a terminal in the project folder; after that, start Claude Code or Codex there. The app does not show whether it is connected.
- AIs read your annotations. Choose a type: Question or Revision for something you want answered or fixed, Note for a remark to yourself. To hand a passage to your AI directly, select it and press Quote, then paste.
- To annotate, turn on the pen+Ab button first; then a selection is marked and you can type a comment.
- An AI's suggested edits wait in Review › Your Changes. Keep writes them into your file; loom saves the previous version first, and you undo a Keep from The Record › Versions › Restore.
- Accept means "I have read this result and vouch for it"; Review › Your Changes lists the results you have never accepted or that changed since.
- Sessions are your AI conversations: their transcripts and what each did are kept in The Record. (I never learned what choosing the session I am "writing in" changes; the introduction would need to say.)
- Problems lists what loom finds wrong in the files; most fixes happen in your editor, some need a terminal, and an open lemma without a proof is expected.
- Starting a new project, pairing with Overleaf and some fixes are done in a terminal, not in the app.
