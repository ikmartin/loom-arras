# 0.19 — viewer speed

## Context

The author reported on 2026-10-11 that "everything is incredibly laggy, even moving the dividing line in split view". The study of the same day (`docs/reports/viewer-speed-study.md`) measured twenty actions on a copy of relloc's `main-atomic` (316 nodes, 1,247 formulas, 43,800 elements) served by `loom serve`, and found three causes:

- **The divider** costs about 115 ms per pointer move: the ratio is an inherited custom property, `--ratio`, on the element holding both panes, so every move restyles both documents and re-lays them out with nothing reusable. Writing the first pane's width directly costs 17.5 ms.
- **Every re-render leaks the document it replaced**: MathJax keeps every formula it has typeset in a list arras never clears, and each entry holds its node. 75,000 nodes and 15 MB per re-render, through garbage collection.
- **Every rebuild re-renders every open document**: a new manifest re-mounts each fragment, and an annotation's one new `<mark>` replaces 43,800 elements and typesets 1,247 formulas again, in a freeze of 350–700 ms for a reader deep in the paper, after two one-second polls.

The author decided on 2026-10-11:
- one plan, three phases, carried out in order;
- a commit per phase, each arras commit followed by its re-vendor;
- the content-visibility change as a trial with pass criteria, reverted if any fails.

The outcome, measured by the live perf suite below: a divider move within 25 ms and frames within 33 ms; live DOM nodes flat across five re-renders; an agent's annotation drawn within a second of `loom annotate` returning, with no task over 100 ms; a return to a document within 150 ms; the graph opening with no task over 100 ms.

## 0.19.1 · The divider, the leak, the freeze

### The work

#### 1. Size the pane, not the workspace
`Workspace.svelte` writes the first pane's width as a literal `flex` and drops `--ratio`. A drag keeps a live ratio, applied at most once a frame, and commits `prefs.divider` on the drop, so the preferences are applied and stored once. `workspace.resizing` is true during a drag: `PdfDoc` holds its drawing and scales the page holders with CSS until the drop, and `CompareLayer` owes its redraw until then.
- **Tests:** pane 0 carries a percentage width and `<main>` no `--ratio`; the divider's snap, keys, Home and double-click (`workspace.e2e.ts:439-477`); a PDF redraws at the fitting zoom after a width change (`e2e-reading/reading.e2e.ts:17-26`).

#### 2. Prune MathJax's list
`run()` in `math/mathjax.ts` removes the entries whose node has left the document before it typesets. Every typeset goes through `run()`, so annotation boxes and previews are covered too; `scoped.ts` builds a document that is thrown away.
- **Tests:** after leaving for the graph and coming back twice, MathJax's list holds as many entries as the page holds formulas.

#### 3. Yield between every batch
`typeset()`'s first part gives the browser a turn between batches, as the rest already does, and stops when its element has left the document. It still resolves only once everything above the reader is set.
- **Tests:** the every-formula-typeset waits and the typeset count of `annotations.e2e.ts:736-761` pass.

### What the book owes
10.1 (batches of 48, each yielding), 15.2.4 (the ratio is the first pane's width; a drag holds a PDF's drawing and the comparison until the drop). DR-335-ikmartin.

## 0.19.2 · A rebuild does not rebuild the page

### The work

#### 4. Patch the fragment instead of replacing it
Mark and include listeners find their fragment and its options when they fire, so DOM that stays across a patch, or moves into a new fragment, keeps working. A fragment manages its own children: on new HTML for the same path it compares the two by their `[data-src]` blocks, replaces only the blocks that differ, syncs the offsets of blocks that only moved, and typesets and wires only what it replaced. It falls back to a full replace when the structure differs, an include is expanded, or a comparison has swapped blocks.
- **Tests:** unit tests of the patch (a mark added, offsets shifted, structure changed, repeated `data-src`); end to end, an annotation filed from the command line leaves an unchanged block's element in place, draws its mark, and typesets nothing else.

#### 5. A manifest that leaves the HTML alone re-wires
A new manifest with unchanged HTML refreshes marks, counts and open boxes without re-creating the comments controller or typesetting; the full re-wiring of comments runs only when what places them changed. Components that typeset titles and statements depend on the macros' value, not on the manifest object.
- **Tests:** a new manifest with unchanged HTML runs no document typeset; a reply draft survives it; the place is kept within 4 px.

#### 6. A stream instead of two polls
`loom serve` offers `/_api/stream`, which says only that a new manifest exists, and its watcher looks at loom's own records five times a second. The viewer listens when the stream is offered and polls otherwise; a static host is unchanged.
- **Tests:** the stream speaks after a rebuild and closes on shutdown; a record append is seen within half a second; a CLI annotation reaches the page within 1.5 s.

#### 7. Coming back to a document costs nothing
A fragment that is destroyed keeps its typeset DOM, for the last two documents, and a fragment made again for the same document takes it back, then patches it if the HTML has moved on. A tab switch, a narrow layout's focus change, and a return from the graph all become a re-wire.
- **Tests:** a tab switch and a return from the graph run no document typeset and keep the place; marks open their boxes afterwards.

What the plan did not foresee:
- **A return still costs about 330 ms**, against 150. What remains is one task of 240–295 ms in which the browser styles and lays out the 43,800 elements put back; nothing in arras runs in it. Only drawing less of the document can cut it, which is the content-visibility trial (0.19.3).
- **The wiring wrote on every pass**: a tone on every environment, every reference's href, every mark's title, tab index and classes, and (briefly, in this phase) every margin, each write making the browser restyle what it touched. It now writes only what differs, which is what lets a patch, and a new manifest, cost nothing a reader sees.
- **Re-wiring after a new manifest must not take placements out first**: a box a reader resolved from stood on a label mark that the reset removed. Placements are taken out only where the markup is replaced.
- **A kept document is back before the pane begins restoring the reader's place**, which then undid an arrival a link asked for. An arrival now tells the pane, which stops restoring, and a kept document answers its request a frame later.
- The patch is proved end to end rather than by unit tests: the unit tier runs without a DOM.

**Tests added:** `e2e-write/patch.e2e.ts` (an annotation from the CLI is drawn into the open document within 1.5 s, an untouched block keeps its element, nothing outside the changed block is typeset, the new mark opens its box; an edit keeps the later blocks of its file and moves their offsets); `workspace.e2e.ts` "coming back to a document" (a tab switch and a return from the graph take the document back with nothing typeset, and its marks open their boxes); `test_serve.py` (the stream speaks after each publication and ends on stop; the watcher sees an append to the annotation log between full scans, and still scans the tree on its interval). ## 0.19.3 · The rest

### The work

#### 8. The graph's layout off the main thread, and only when its inputs change
The Dots layout is keyed by its nodes and edges, runs in a worker, and is guarded against arriving after a later request; the local graph restarts only when its neighbourhood changes.
- **Tests:** the key; `graph.e2e.ts`; opening the graph runs no task over 100 ms.

#### 9. A PDF's scroll reads its pages once a frame
`PdfDoc.scrolled` runs at most once a frame against cached page offsets.
- **Tests:** `e2e-reading`; forty wheel steps run fewer than 100 layouts.

#### 10. content-visibility, as a trial
On the read view's leaf blocks, with `contain-intrinsic-size: auto`. Kept only if nothing is clipped, ten far jumps land within 4 px, the place holds through a rebuild and a patch, and size and format changes are at least twice as fast; otherwise reverted, and the reason recorded here.

### What the book owes
