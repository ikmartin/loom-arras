The design is settled in [compare-study.md](../reports/compare-study.md) and summarised as decisions 20–26 in [0.17-ai-drafting-and-compare.md](0.17-ai-drafting-and-compare.md), whose "final compare design" section holds the screenshots this plan builds to (`images/0.17/`, with the demo page as `demo.html`).

## Settled in planning

- **Compare's texts are rendered by loom** at build *(to confirm with the author)*, since the word diff and the change marks are loom's (`render/review_compare.py:22-57`; `render/convert.py:367`, `:507`). Every node element in every fragment — draft, agent copy, landmark — gains `data-pair`, its key with any derived suffix stripped, and `data-hash`, its text's `mathematical_hash` (decision 28), beside the attributes it has (`render/fragments.py:358-422`); landmarks still carry no `data-key` or `data-id`, so no live wiring reaches them. The manifest gains `compare.pairs`, one entry per pair of differing texts some comparison can form — each derived node against its counterpart, each key's recorded versions against each other and against today — keyed by the two hashes, each with its two highlighted fragments, its direction (which side is the base, both changed since the base, or none) and its macro sets. Identical pairs render once. A pair differing only in a display name has no highlighted words and shows the name changed.
- **Compare composes in arras.** For the two focused items it pairs nodes by `data-pair`, looks up each differing pair by its hashes, and swaps the node's body in each pane for the pair's highlighted rendering, typeset with its own macros (`isolatedMacros`, `arras/src/lib/fragments/Fragment.svelte:187`). Presence and order it computes itself, from the order of `data-pair` in the two documents: the nodes outside a longest common subsequence are the ones marked, "moved" when one side is a copy of the other (`copy_of`), "order differs" otherwise. The gutter is drawn per pane over the pane's scroller (`PaneContext`, `workspace/state.svelte.ts:43`).
- **A wedge stands where the base places a node**: after the partner of the nearest preceding node that pairs, and at the document's head when none precedes it.
- **A node item may show a recorded version** *(to confirm with the author)*, since "two versions of one key" is a comparison and nothing opens `key@N` today (`workspace/item.ts:10-39`): a node item takes a `version` parameter, addressed `/node/<key>@<step>`, rendered from a fragment loom writes for every recorded version, with that version's macros.
- **The filter's `off`** is a third value of `sessionView.view` (`sessions/sessions.svelte.ts:23`), kept by `load()` (`:34`) and answered by `visible()` (`:99`), with a third button in `SessionFilter.svelte` (`:23-25`). The value compare displaced is saved in the same record, so a reload while comparing still restores it on leaving; compare itself is in the workspace's URL beside `?beside` (`workspace/store.svelte.ts:169`).
- **Compare is disabled below the narrow width** (`Workspace.svelte:21`), where only one pane is drawn, and while either focused item is a paper, a session or a node's context; its title says which.
- **Keys `[` and `]`** are bound by the rail while comparing, ignoring modifiers and typing, since `e`, `h` and `s` live in the focused fragment (`Fragment.svelte:294`) and a step concerns both panes.
- **Double-click** is handled on each fragment's root while comparing: the node is `closest('[data-pair]')`, a double-click on an annotation mark keeps its own meaning (`fragments/mount.ts:229`), and the flash is `travel.ts`'s (`:18`).
- **Names:** where a node has a display name (DR-301-luisa), compare's tags and the counter's tooltip use it, as Graph and Review do.

## Behaviour that must survive

- The annotation filter's `this session` and `all` behave as today.
- A landmark carries no live wiring.
- Review's own comparisons (a stale key, an incoming change) are unchanged.

## Risks

- **The pair count** grows with every key's versions: each key contributes a pair per two distinct recorded texts. Identical pairs render once, and `demos/relloc` (57 keys, five referee runs) is the measure; if it is too many, pairs between two old versions are rendered on demand by the serving publisher instead.
