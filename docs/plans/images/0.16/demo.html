Document Controls

Drafts of the look for a document's controls once they leave the rail. The first round's A, B and C are critiqued below, and the critiques are turned into requirements; the three drafts after them are the revision. Compare now sits on the rail, because it concerns both documents on screen rather than one; it is a dummy until 0.17. When the control appears and fades is still left for later.

The first round, critiqued

A · a line of the page

  1. It cannot float. It has no surface of its own, so over scrolled text the words show through it. Floating, it has to grow a background, and then it is B.
  2. It reads as the paper's. A rule across the text column looks like a running head, which blurs P1's line between the document and the application.
  3. It moves the text. It sits in the flow, so appearing, wrapping in a narrow pane, or fading all move the text beneath; drawn on the focused pane only, a change of focus moves one pane's text.
  4. A mode is only a word. Whether settled annotations are shown is legible only as show or hide.

B · a pill

  1. Centred is the wrong place. The title and the section headings are centred, so a centred control lands on the lines a reader orients by.
  2. "show all" lost its noun, and with it half its question (P4); kept in full, the pill is long.
  3. The shadow is not the objection it seemed. The book says "no shadows", yet the floating box and the local graph's toggle both cast one: in practice, what floats over the text casts one.
  4. Two pills, two jobs. The rail's filter is a pill too; two like shapes that do unlike things invite confusion.
  5. It grows per item. A node adds context and verbatim code, and the pill stretches across the page.

C · a corner stack

  1. Icons must be learned. Show all and show settled have no established glyph, so the reader asks what a button is before what it does (P4); a tooltip needs a hover, and keyboard and touch have none.
  2. State is a wash on a sixteen-pixel icon.
  3. It is tall. Six rows in a narrow pane reach down beside the first paragraph.
  4. The corner is right. Off the centre line, opposite the local graph's toggle, and taking the least of the text column (P1).

What the critiques ask of the revision

  • A surface of its own, since it will float (A1).
  • Anchored at a corner, never on the centre line (B1, C4).
  • Words in full, except the two tools, whose glyphs are established (B2, C1).
  • One line, never a column (C3).
  • Standing over the page, not in its flow, so the text never moves for it (A3).
  • On the focused pane only, which answers F4 without a second copy.

A′

A's words kept, and given the surface A lacked: a thin strip along the focused pane's top edge, under its tabs, across the pane rather than the text column. It is chrome, drawn as the pane's own, so nothing about it looks printed.

C′, final: how it floats

The fade is 0.4 s and the region is a band across the top of the pane, fixed in size rather than a proportion of it. The band is h tall from the top of the pane and inset from the pane's sides by a padding that differs left and right. It always contains the controls: the right padding stops short of them, and the band is never shorter than they reach, whatever the values.
  • The controls show while the pointer is inside the band and fade when it leaves; they stay while the pointer is on them, while the view menu is open, and while the pin is on.
  • Keyboard focus inside the pane brings them back.
  • The dashed yellow band is drawn here only to show the region, and would not be drawn in arras.

A band 96px tall across the top of the pane, inset 96px from the left and 4px from the right, wakes the controls; they fade over 0.4 s when the pointer leaves it. Drawn on the focused pane only. Widgets, gadgets, and their fixed loci

1 Introduction

This paper studies widgets. Definition 1.1. A widget is a pair of a set and an involution of it, so that its fixed locus is well defined; every orbit of a widget has one or two points.

The basic identity is that the involution squares to the identity. From it, a point is either fixed or paired with exactly one other point, and the count of points splits accordingly into fixed points and pairs.

2 Results

Theorem 2.1. Let X be a finite widget. Then the number of points of X and the number of points of its fixed locus have the same parity.

Proof. The points that are not fixed come in pairs, by Lemma 1.2, so removing them changes the count by an even number. What remains is the fixed locus.

The theorem fails for infinite widgets, where pairs can no longer be counted, and the appendix gives an incomplete argument for a weaker statement in that case.

controls: showing

Every toolbar, by item

The finalized C′ on every kind of item a pane can hold. Only the focused pane ever shows its toolbar; the other pane shows none, whatever it holds. Each card is shown pinned, the default; the pin is one preference shared by every toolbar, saved when it changes and kept between visits. Turn it off on any card and all of them fade under the finalized rule. The rail holds only what concerns both panes: the annotation filter and compare.
  • On the bar: the item's views as a dropdown named for the one showing, where it has views; the two annotating tools, where a publisher is serving; and anything used continually while reading.
  • Under view: everything else, each in full words with its key.
  • An item with no controls and no views gets no bar.
