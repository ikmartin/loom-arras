What each draft is trying

  1. A · Marks only. The two documents as they read today, with the change marks and nothing else.
  2. B · Holes. A, plus a one-line placeholder where a node exists only on the other side.
  3. C · Sameness folded. Nodes that match fold to a single line, so what is left is what differs.
  4. D · Stepping from the rail. A, plus a difference counter on the rail whose arrows bring both panes to the next differing pair.
  5. E · A seam between the panes. A, plus the divider widened into a strip that draws a band between each pair of partners in view.
  6. F · A git gutter. In place of A's rules, red and green line markers down each pane's left edge, as git and editors draw a diff.

Questions this round raises

  1. Double-click also selects a word, and the select tool (0.15) turns a selection into an annotation. The jump could clear the selection it makes, as these drafts do, or use ⌘-click (the usual SyncTeX gesture in PDF viewers, and free in arras today), or both.
  2. Which drafts combine. B, C, D and E are each an addition to A; D and B, or D and C, are natural pairs. E is the most drawing and the least P6. F replaces A's marks rather than adding to them, and composes with B, C and D in the same way A does.
  3. Whether direction is ever shown. Every draft is symmetric — neither side is "old" — because compare serves any two versions. An agent copy against its source has a direction; the marks could say "new in aidoc.tex" rather than "not in main.tex". F is the one draft that takes a direction, by its colours.

Against the principles

Each draft measured against P1–P6 (plans 0.13.3 and 0.14) and the annotation principles A1–A6 (the annotation study, plan 0.15). Two findings apply to every draft and are not repeated in the rows. P2: the rail's "main.tex ↔ aidoc.tex" restates the two tabs, which already name what is compared; the names go. A4: annotation marks own the wash (which box is open) and the hues (red is an objection, ochre a suggestion); every draft washes changed words at rest in the stale amber, which sits beside the suggestion's ochre and shares the wash of an open annotation — so on a page carrying both, a changed phrase and an annotated one look alike. The drafts above draw three annotations (an objection and a question in main.tex, a suggestion in aidoc.tex) to show it: compare Lemma 1.2's "one or" and the suggestion beside "come in pairs".

Recommendation

A's marks and D's counter, with the annotation filter gaining off and compare choosing it. Turning annotations off removes A4's collision, which was the one reason to hold the word differences back; in compare mode the differences are the thing the reader came for.

  1. The filter gains off: this session · all · off, available at any time. Pressing compare chooses off; releasing compare restores what was chosen before. The reader may choose again while comparing.
  2. At rest while comparing: Review's rule (review-change-point) at the left of each node that differs, a place annotations never draw since a block annotation is underlined, not barred; its changed words washed on both sides; a dashed rule and "not in aidoc.tex" where the other document lacks the node. No holes, no folds, no gutter, no seam.
  3. The rail while comparing: the filter, then compare pressed with "5 differences" and ‹ › beside it (keys [ and ]), which reads "2 of 5" once stepped. No document names: the tabs say them.
  4. Finding a partner: double-click a node and its partner comes to the same height and flashes, clearing the word selection the double-click makes; hover lights the partner. The panes never scroll together.
  5. Symmetric: no red, no green, no old and new; the tag names the other document. Prose between nodes is unmarked.

Dropped on the way: opening one pair at a time to show its words (it answered A4 by hiding the answer; the filter answers it by clearing the page).

Refined: F's marks where a base is known, R's where none is

P3 applied both ways: show a direction exactly when loom knows one. Moves, which every earlier draft but E missed, are marked in both.

A base is known — use F's marks
  • an agent copy against its source: each -ai node records its base
  • a landmark against today, or two landmarks: ordered in time
  • two versions of one key, key@N
No base — use R's marks
  • two of the author's documents, main.tex and talk.tex
  • shared nodes are one node file, so the same text: only presence and order can differ
  • so R's word washes never appear here; they belong to the based case, which F now draws
