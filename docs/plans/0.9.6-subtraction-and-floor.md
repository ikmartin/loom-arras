# 0.9.6 — The subtraction and the floor

## Context

Two publishers are coming: **weft**, a corpus tool for other people's results at library scale, and the author's **sitegen**. Both change what loom and arras owe the world, in opposite directions.

loom owes them *space*. Its reference crawl — `src/loom/refs/crawl/`, three commands, the `[crawl]` table, 1,406 lines built on 2026-09-17 — is corpus machinery living in a paper's tool. `docs/plans/weft-and-loom.md` settles that it goes to weft, and §5 lists what loom keeps: the bibliography and its identifiers, `refs resolve`, `refs add`, `refs path`, `digest fetch` at depth 1, extraction, digests, bundles, and the whole review ledger. What loom gains is a smaller promise: no database, no service clients beyond the two lookups, no rate budget, and a surface entirely about the paper in front of you.

arras owes them *tolerance*. Plan 0.9.5 §4(a) found that every top-level manifest section must be present or the app throws at boot, and §10 asked for a fixture of the interface's floor, belonging to the specification and generated by nobody's publisher. With one hypothetical second publisher that was debt-payment; with two real ones it is a prerequisite, because neither can render anything until it lands.

Those are one plan because they are one act: **loom stops being a corpus tool, and arras stops assuming its corpus is loom's.** Neither adds a feature. Both are the boundary becoming real before anyone leans on it.

R1 of 0.9.5 landed on 2026-09-17 (`arras a0a00d7`, DR-143). This plan carries R2a. R2b and R3 stay where 0.9.5's weave puts them, in 0.11 and WQ-31.

**Decided with the author while planning:**
- The crawl is deleted **now**; weft copies from git history, and the removal's decision record names the commit.
- **No digest specification.** §4 of weft-and-loom asks for a digest contract "both implementations must keep", but a digest is a quilt artifact — an abbreviated cited paper under your citekey, with your locators and a place in your ledger — and weft produces no such thing. §3 of the same document says "two digests of one paper may differ, and that is correct", which removes the invariant a contract would protect. weft forks `digest/extract.py` and owns its divergence. §4 is amended to say so.
- **WQ-06 closes.** Its size clause ("`refs/` across all quilts exceeds a few GB") cannot fire at depth 1, and the item's own Related line concedes that the 150 GB at depth 5 is "what makes this necessary rather than tidy".
- **R2a lands here**, not in 0.10's body as 0.9.5 §6 wrote: unblocking two publishers beats 0.10's file locality.

## Scope

**In:** the crawl's deletion from loom, with its config, tests, scripts and documentation; the queue re-read it forces; the amendment to weft-and-loom §4; arras booting on a manifest with sections absent; `fixture-minimal` as a spec-owned artifact with a suite that runs against it; two base-path gaps R1 left.

**Out:** anything weft does in its own repository. The vocabulary of 0.9.5 §4(b) and the `publishes` capability block (R2b, 0.11). The host split (WQ-31). `interface_version` stays 1 — everything here is additive or subtractive at the publisher's end, and nothing changes what a conforming manifest means.

## Part B — the floor (arras and the specification)

### B1. Absence tolerance

Seventeen top-level sections are required today; only `canon` and `relations` are optional. Nine sites throw on the first render of any route and two more on particular routes, and there is no `+error.svelte`, so an absent section blanks the app. The sites are spread over `views.ts`, `+layout.svelte`, `Palette.svelte`, `badges.ts`, `graph/layout.ts`, `mount.ts`, `nav.ts`, `reached.ts`, `worklink.ts`, `LinkPreview.svelte` and the graph modules — around forty expressions.

**Do it in one place, not forty.** `parseManifest` (`src/lib/manifest/loader.ts:44-63`) currently ends in an unchecked `data as Manifest`. Replace that with a `normalise()` that fills every absent top-level section with its empty value — `[]`, `{}`, `{default: [], sets: {}}`, `{labels: {}, derived: {}}`, `{name: '', root_label: ''}` — and returns a Manifest. Every consumer then keeps its present shape and its types, and the guarantee lives where the boundary is: the publisher may omit, the viewer normalises.

Two consequences to write down rather than discover:

- `types.ts` keeps the sections required, because after normalisation they are. The **specification** is what changes: `docs/specs/manifest.md` gains a statement that a publisher may omit any top-level section it has nothing to say about, and a viewer treats an absent section as empty. That is additive and does not touch `interface_version`.
- `scripts/build-prerender.mjs` runs outside the app and reads the manifest directly (`:16`, no version check; `:20-26`, six unguarded sections, one of them a `for…of`). It needs its own guards — the smallest honest fix is to import nothing and default each section inline.

### B2. `fixture-minimal`

A hand-built manifest at `docs/specs/fixture-minimal/manifest.json`, owned by the specification and generated by nobody's publisher, vendored into `arras/tests/fixture-minimal/`. It is not touched by `refresh-fixture.sh`, which generates the other fixture from loom's synthetic quilt; `fixture.md` gains a section saying the two have different jobs and must not be merged.

Contents, per 0.9.5 §10, with one refinement: sections in the "deliberately empty" list are **omitted outright** rather than written as `{}`, because absence is what B1 widens the floor for and `{}` would pass today.

- present and non-empty: `interface_version`, `publisher`, `corpus`, `nodes`, `edges`, `tags`, `taxa`, `search`, `macros`, `states`
- omitted: `masters`, `keys`, `regions`, `inclusion`, `annotations`, `threads`, `references`, `diagnostics`, `generated`
- deliberately strange: a state label no viewer knows, a diagnostic code no viewer knows (in a node's state, since `diagnostics` is omitted — or a single-entry `diagnostics` if that reads better in execution), a relation kind no viewer knows, and a node whose taxon appears nowhere else

Its nodes name fragments, so a handful of minimal fragment files ship beside the manifest or the node pages show fetch failures rather than empty states.

### B3. The suite that runs against it

The existing e2e suite cannot be re-pointed: it hard-codes `sy-0003`, `Kre99`, `widgets-v1`, `'5 stale'` and two dozen other fixture facts. What §11 checks 1 and 2 actually describe is a new, assertion-light suite:

- `arras/tests/e2e-minimal/minimal.e2e.ts`: visit every route the manifest can name plus the fixed ones, assert a rendered `<main>`, no console error, and no unhandled rejection.
- The vocabulary assertion of check 2 — no page contains "not yet compiled", "loose", or a publisher's name — is written **now** and scoped with a named `PENDING_R2B` list, exactly as `tests/unit/host-neutrality.spec.ts` does for the `loom:` scheme. R2a cannot satisfy it (those phrases are 0.9.5 §4(b), which 0.11 carries), and a test that names what it does not yet check is better than one written later.

Mechanics: `scripts/stage-fixture.mjs` already takes the directory as `argv[2]`, so only the npm script pins `tests/fixture`. Add `build:fixture-minimal` and a second Playwright config on **a different port** — `playwright.config.ts` and `playwright.shots.config.ts` both pin 4173 with `reuseExistingServer: false`, so a third suite on 4173 cannot run beside them. `playwright.shots.config.ts` is the pattern to copy. CI (`arras/.github/workflows/ci.yml:21`) gains one step.

### B4. Two gaps R1 left

Both are real base-path bugs, found while mapping B1:

- `src/routes/blockers/+page.ts:5` — `redirect(308, '/review?show=incomplete')` is not composed from `base`, so `/blockers` under a prefix redirects out of the app.
- `src/lib/shell/RailSections.svelte:16` and `src/lib/shell/IconStrip.svelte:36` — `<a href="/" class="name">`. The host-neutrality guard's route rule requires a lowercase letter after the slash, so a bare `href="/"` slips through. Fix both and tighten the regex.

## Ordering

Part A and Part B touch different repositories and can be done in either order. Within them:

1. **A1–A3** (code, config, tests) in one pass, with `ruff` and `mypy` as the completeness check for the deletion.
2. **A4–A6** (book, queue, weft-and-loom) once the code is gone, so the book is edited against what exists.
3. **B1** first of Part B: `fixture-minimal` cannot boot until absence is tolerated.
4. **B2, B3** together; **B4** anywhere.

The whole of Part A is subtraction plus documentation and should be one commit; Part B is one more.
