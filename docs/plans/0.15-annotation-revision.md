# 0.15 — the annotation revision

Plan for revising the annotation system, from the study in `docs/reports/annotation-study.md`, settled with the author on 2026-09-24 and 2026-09-25. The study's part one is the inventory and the definition, part two the critique and the fix list, and §4 and §9 its twelve decisions; this plan builds them. Each phase is planned in plan mode, implemented, verified with `scripts/verify full` and stopped for review. Phases are recorded under Delivery.

## Context

An annotation is a record of (kind, target, anchor, state), and how it looks and how it is used are to be derived from that record. Today they are not: the viewer draws 24 styles in 22 colours for six kinds, a resolved annotation looks like an open one, five nouns name one record, and every open annotation is drawn in colour at rest, so a reviewed page stays as busy as an unreviewed one. The study's fix list has 23 items; this plan delivers them in six phases, the data model first, since the viewer's changes depend on what the manifest carries.

## The decisions (study §4, §9)

1. The record is (kind, target, anchor, state); styling and interaction are functions of it.
2. `confirmation` merges into `note`; the kinds are objection, suggestion, question, citation, note.
3. Settled means resolved or discarded, marked by hand, for every kind.
4. Settled annotations are hidden at rest; shown, they keep their hue at a 3% wash with the underline at half weight and 50% opacity, the words untouched.
5. Hues: objection red, suggestion yellow, question blue, citation violet, note neutral.
6. An open note is neutral at the middle weight.
7. Severity is the underline's weight and nothing else; the wash is one strength.
8. "annotation" is the one noun: `loom annotate`, `loom ai annotations`.
9. A node annotation may name the document it is read in, marked there, listed on the node's page, absent elsewhere.
10. `loom refs note` becomes `loom refs cite`.
11. The document is written with `--in DOC` and the API's `in`; the viewer implies it.
12. Settled annotations are shown by a rail control held for the session, with a key beside `e` and `h`.

## Settled in planning

- **Loom bakes the marks.** `render/build.py:276 _marks_by_node` puts every open-or-not mark into the fragment HTML, and the viewer wires what it finds. So decisions 4, 9 and fix 11 (the session filter) each need both sides: loom emits no mark for a settled annotation or for one whose document is not the one being rendered, and the viewer hides marks it wires when the filter or the settled control says so. The manifest carries the full record either way, so the viewer can draw a settled mark when asked without a rebuild: loom emits settled marks with a `data-settled` attribute and the viewer hides them by default, rather than omitting them, since a rebuild on every resolve is the wrong cost.
- **No compatibility for `confirmation`.** Existing logs saying `confirmation` are read as `note` by replay, which is one line in `records/log.py`, and the three fixture annotations that carry it are regenerated as notes. The no-backwards-compatibility rule applies to everything else: no aliases for `loom comment`, `ai findings` or `refs note`.
- **The write API endpoint `refs-note` becomes `refs-cite`** with the command, and `comment` becomes `annotate`; the spec, arras's `write.ts` and the tests follow. CAPABILITIES is the list; `test_write_api.py` fails when a capability lacks a case.
- **The viewer's persisted `comments` preference** (floating or inline) keeps its stored key, since renaming it costs a migration for one word nobody sees; its settings label becomes "Annotations".
- **One `style()` in CSS, not in code.** The kind class on a mark (`k-objection`) stays as the seam; what changes is that one block of `theme.css` styles every surface from five hue tokens and three weights, and the per-surface blocks go. Severity becomes a class too (`s-major`), so weight is CSS.
- **Fixture regeneration is part of every phase that touches the record:** `gen_quilts.py`, `refresh-fixture.sh`, `demos/build.py`, and `vendor_arras.py` at the end, as in 0.14.1.

## Behaviour that must survive

- Every annotation in a log written before this plan is read: `confirmation` as `note`, the rest unchanged.
- A reply copies its parent's target, anchor and document.
- An agent's links are still validated; the packet still carries only a person's annotations; the author's verbs still refuse an agent.
- The conformance fixture's shape changes only by `in` and the merged kind.
- The write suite's real-publisher flows (edit, discard, undo, `refs cite`, review-finish) pass end to end.

## Risks

- **The baked marks.** Filtering by `in` and status at build time means a resolve does not change a fragment until the next build; `data-settled` on every mark and the viewer hiding them is what keeps the page right between builds. Phase 1 must test both a cold build and a mark resolved after it.
- **The rename touches ~110 test call sites.** Done mechanically in one pass with the harness's `ok("annotate", …)`, the count before and after equal.
- **Accept and reject move into the box.** The Library's suggested-citations list loses its verbs; check nothing else offered them.
