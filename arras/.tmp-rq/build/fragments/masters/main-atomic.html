Balanced flows on finite quivers

We compute the rank of the lattice of balanced weightings of a finite quiver, show that it is a saturated sublattice of the weighting lattice, and count its bounded members. Nothing here is true by accident: the quiver is a showcase.

Introduction

A quiver carries two lattices: the weightings of its arrows, and the balanced weightings, those that neither create nor destroy anything at a vertex. This paper is about the second. Our main result, Theorem sh-0009, computes its rank from the combinatorics of the underlying graph alone, and Theorem sh-000C counts the balanced weightings of bounded height.

The cycle lattice of a quiver is classical, and we follow [Arden24, Proposition 2.1] throughout; the counting argument of Section sh-0300 is an adaptation of the median-order technique of [Bellamy19, Theorem 2.3]. We also use the exchange bound of [Arden24, Lemma 5.9], which the digest of that paper does not carry. The survey [Cortez07] lists what is known for infinite quivers, none of which we use.

Convention

Every quiver in this paper is finite: both \(V(\quiv)\) and \(A(\quiv)\) are finite sets, and loops and parallel arrows are allowed, a loop counting as one arrow and contributing no divergence. All lattices are free abelian groups of finite rank, written additively.

Weightings and the divergence map

Definition (Quiver)

A quiver \(\quiv\) is a quadruple \((V, A, s, t)\) with \(V\) and \(A\) finite sets and \(s, t \colon A \to V\) the source and target maps. A weighting of \(\quiv\) is a function \(w \colon A \to \ZZ\), and the weightings form a lattice \(W(\quiv) \cong \ZZ^{A}\) under pointwise addition.

Definition (Divergence)

The divergence of a weighting \(w\) at a vertex \(v\) is

\[(\bd w)(v) \;=\; \sum_{a \colon t(a) = v} w(a) \;-\; \sum_{a \colon s(a) = v} w(a),\]

the weight arriving at \(v\) less the weight leaving it. The resulting homomorphism \(\bd \colon W(\quiv) \to \ZZ^{V}\) is the divergence map.

Definition (Balanced weighting)

A weighting \(w\) is balanced if \(\bd w = 0\), and the balanced weightings form the sublattice

\[\Zcyc(\quiv) \;=\; \ker \bd \;\subseteq\; W(\quiv),\]

which we call the cycle lattice of \(\quiv\).

Example

For the quiver with one vertex and \(n\) loops, every weighting is balanced and \(\Zcyc(\quiv) = \ZZ^{n}\). For a single arrow between two distinct vertices, only the zero weighting is balanced.

Lemma (Support)

Let \(w\) be a balanced weighting with \(w \geq 0\) and \(w \neq 0\). Then the support \(\supp(w)\) contains a directed cycle of \(\quiv\).

Proof

Pick \(a_{0}\) with \(w(a_{0}) > 0\). Since \((\bd w)(t(a_{0})) = 0\) and the arrow \(a_{0}\) contributes positively to that sum, some arrow \(a_{1}\) with \(s(a_{1}) = t(a_{0})\) has \(w(a_{1}) > 0\). Repeating, we obtain an infinite walk inside \(\supp(w)\), which is a finite set by Convention sh-0001, so some vertex repeats and the walk contains a directed cycle.

Proposition (Saturation)

\(\Zcyc(\quiv)\) is a saturated sublattice of \(W(\quiv)\): if \(n w \in \Zcyc(\quiv)\) for some \(w \in W(\quiv)\) and some integer \(n \neq 0\), then \(w \in \Zcyc(\quiv)\).

Proof First proof

The divergence map is a homomorphism into \(\ZZ^{V}\), which is torsion-free, so \(\bd(n w) = n \, \bd w = 0\) forces \(\bd w = 0\).

Proof Second proof of Proposition sh-0007

The quotient \(W(\quiv) / \Zcyc(\quiv)\) embeds in \(\ZZ^{V}\) by the first isomorphism theorem, hence is torsion-free, and a sublattice with torsion-free quotient is saturated. The cycle lattice is the kernel of Definition sh-0004, so nothing else is needed.

Remark

Saturation is what makes the rank in Theorem sh-0009 an honest dimension count: over \(\RR\) the same kernel has the same rank, and no index creeps in.

The rank of the cycle lattice

Theorem (Rank)

Let \(\quiv\) be a quiver whose underlying graph has \(c\) connected components. Then

\[\rank \Zcyc(\quiv) \;=\; |A(\quiv)| - |V(\quiv)| + c.\]
Proof

The image of \(\bd\) is the set of integer vectors summing to zero on each component, a sublattice of \(\ZZ^{V}\) of rank \(|V(\quiv)| - c\); this is [Arden24, Proposition 2.1]. Since \(\Zcyc(\quiv) = \ker \bd\) is saturated by Proposition sh-0007, the rank of the kernel is the rank of the source less the rank of the image, which gives (eq:rank).

Corollary (Trees)

A quiver whose underlying graph is a forest has \(\Zcyc(\quiv) = 0\).

Proof

A forest on \(|V(\quiv)|\) vertices with \(c\) components has exactly \(|V(\quiv)| - c\) edges, so Theorem sh-0009 and (eq:rank) give rank zero, and a saturated sublattice of rank zero is trivial.

Bounded balanced weightings

Definition (Height)

The height of a weighting \(w\) is \(\|w\| = \max_{a \in A(\quiv)} |w(a)|\), and \(N_{\quiv}(k)\) denotes the number of balanced weightings of height at most \(k\).

Theorem (Counting)

For every quiver \(\quiv\) there is a polynomial \(P_{\quiv}\) of degree \(\rank \Zcyc(\quiv)\) with \(N_{\quiv}(k) = P_{\quiv}(k)\) for all \(k \geq 0\).

Proof

The balanced weightings of height at most \(k\) are the lattice points of the dilate \(k \Pi\) of the polytope \(\Pi = \Zcyc(\quiv) \otimes \RR \cap [-1,1]^{A}\), which is a rational polytope of dimension \(\rank \Zcyc(\quiv)\) by Theorem sh-0009. Ehrhart’s theorem in the form of [Bellamy19, Theorem 3.2] applies, since \(\Pi\) has integral vertices by the saturation of Proposition sh-0007.

Caveat

The polytope \(\Pi\) need not have integral vertices when parallel arrows are weighted with different signs; the proof above quietly assumes it does, and Section sh-0400 records what is needed.

What is left

Lemma (Integrality)

The vertex description of \(\Pi\) is not written down, so integrality is unproved. The polytope \(\Pi\) of Theorem sh-000C has integral vertices.

Remark

Every statement above is about a finite quiver. For an infinite quiver with finite valence the cycle lattice is still saturated, but its rank is no longer given by (eq:rank); see [Cortez07].
