What a quiver weighs

Definition (Quiver) A quiver \(\beamer@setmathcolor \quiv \) is a quadruple \(\beamer@setmathcolor (V, A, s, t)\) with \(\beamer@setmathcolor V\) and \(\beamer@setmathcolor A\) finite sets and \(\beamer@setmathcolor s, t \colon A \to V\) the source and target maps. A weighting of \(\beamer@setmathcolor \quiv \) is a function \(\beamer@setmathcolor w \colon A \to \ZZ \), and the weightings form a lattice \(\beamer@setmathcolor W(\quiv ) \cong \ZZ ^{A}\) under pointwise addition.

The one lemma

Lemma (Support) Let \(\beamer@setmathcolor w\) be a balanced weighting with \(\beamer@setmathcolor w \geq 0\) and \(\beamer@setmathcolor w \neq 0\). Then \(\beamer@setmathcolor \supp (w)\) contains a directed cycle.

Proof. Pick \(\beamer@setmathcolor a_{0}\) with \(\beamer@setmathcolor w(a_{0}) > 0\). Since \(\beamer@setmathcolor (\bd w)(t(a_{0})) = 0\) and the arrow \(\beamer@setmathcolor a_{0}\) contributes positively to that sum, some arrow \(\beamer@setmathcolor a_{1}\) with \(\beamer@setmathcolor s(a_{1}) = t(a_{0})\) has \(\beamer@setmathcolor w(a_{1}) > 0\). Repeating, we obtain an infinite walk inside \(\beamer@setmathcolor \supp (w)\), which is finite, so some vertex repeats and the walk contains a directed cycle. □
